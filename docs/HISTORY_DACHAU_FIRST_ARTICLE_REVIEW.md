### Original body

On this day, American troops liberated Dachau.  In 1942, Dachau built a crematorium; The concentration camp had been in operation for over a decade, with Jewish prisoners as the significant majority. Dachau initially imprisoned political prisoners and forced them in aiding to construct the camp. While in Dachau, prisoners were subjected to biological experiments against their consent, killing or disabling hundreds of prisoners.

On the days leading up to liberation, thousands of prisoners were on a death march to Tegernsee, Germany. Upon the American arrival, prisoners greeted their rescuers  with flags from the various Allied nations. The Nazi commanders sent a swath of prisoners to Dachau before its liberation. Records estimate that 30,000 people died in Dachau before the American liberation, which managed to rescue 30,000 prisoners.

## 2. Editorial review

Listed, not silently fixed.

### A. Grammar, spelling, punctuation

- Double spaces after “Dachau.” and after “rescuers”.
- Comma splice / broken semicolon: “crematorium; The concentration camp”.
- “On the days leading up” should be “In the days leading up”.
- “forced them in aiding to construct” is ungrammatical.
- Trailing space after the final sentence.

### B. Awkward or unclear writing

- “Dachau built a crematorium” makes the camp the actor; the SS built it.
- “swath of prisoners” is vague.
- “which managed to rescue” treats liberation as a completed rescue count and
  hides post-liberation deaths.
- The 1942 crematorium sentence is dropped into the liberation lede without a
  hinge.

### C. Repetitive wording

- “prisoners” appears eight times in two short paragraphs.
- The 30,000 / 30,000 pairing is rhetorically neat and historically sloppy.

### D. Potentially outdated terminology

- “biological experiments” is less precise than the institutional term
  “medical experiments.”
- “US” in the headline is brand-voice; “U.S.” or “American forces” is the
  more conventional public form.

### E. Claims that appear factual and should be sourced

Every sentence after the opening “On this day…” clause.

### F. Statistics / numbers that require verification

- “significant majority” Jewish
- “hundreds” killed or disabled by experiments
- “thousands” on the Tegernsee death march
- “30,000” dead
- “30,000” rescued / still in the camp

### G. Historical dates that require confirmation

- Liberation date 1945-04-29
- Crematorium / crematoria area in 1942
- “over a decade” of operation
- Death-march timing (“days leading up”)

### H. Names, titles, transliterations

- “Dachau” is correct for the camp and the Bavarian town.
- “Tegernsee” is correct and should be confirmed as the death-march
  destination.
- No personal names appear. Unit names (42nd Infantry Division, 45th Infantry
  Division, 20th Armored Division, Seventh Army) are absent and should not be
  invented into the body without founder approval.

### I. Sensitive wording

- “rescue” can flatten the aftermath: thousands died after April 29 from
  typhus, starvation, and the effects of imprisonment.
- The Jewish-majority claim erases political prisoners, clergy, Soviet
  prisoners of war, and other groups, and is factually wrong for most of the
  camp’s history.
- A liberation story can become a US-triumph piece if the prisoners’ prior
  suffering and the railroad-car dead are omitted. The original does not
  mention the corpse train; that omission is not a defect by itself, but the
  “rescue” close can read as a finished deliverance.

### J. Misleading even if partly true

- “Jewish prisoners as the significant majority” is the most serious
  distortion.
- “30,000 died” / “30,000 rescued” sounds like a closed ledger.
- The death march is described as if it were the liberation scene; those
  marchers were gone. Americans liberated the prisoners who remained, then
  overtook the march in early May.
- “Dachau built a crematorium” compresses an old crematorium and the 1942
  Barrack X complex, which also contained a gas chamber that institutional
  historians say was not used for mass murder at Dachau.

| Nazi authorities sent large numbers of prisoners into Dachau before liberation | VERIFIED | USHMM: evacuation transports from camps in the east; overcrowding and typhus. Memorial: at least 25,000 from the Dachau system were marched or transported away; incoming transports also jammed the main camp. |

USHMM film captions: about 30,000 starving prisoners in the camp. Yad Vashem: more than 60,000 in the camp from more than 30 countries — a broader camp-system figure. “Rescue 30,000” overstates a single completed rescue. |
| Prisoners greeted American troops with flags of various Allied nations | LIKELY BUT NEEDS SOURCE | National WWII Museum, citing the Seventh Army report: inmates showed homemade flags. USHMM holds a homemade American-flag greeting photograph whose catalog record is Allach, a Dachau subcamp, while an older caption says “Dachau prison camp.” “Various Allied nations” is stronger than the sources securely support. |
| “Records estimate that 30,000 people died” | CONFLICTING | Yad Vashem’s older encyclopedia: over 30,000 “officially,” “certainly much higher.” USHMM: scholars believe at least 40,000. Dachau Memorial: approximately 41,500 murdered in the camp, subcamps, and death marches. Civil-register tallies near 32,000 are incomplete. |
| Jewish prisoners were “the significant majority” | CONFLICTING | USHMM: relatively few Jews in the early years; almost 11,000 Jewish men after Kristallnacht, then a sharp decline when Jews were deported east; Jews returned on late-war death marches. On April 26, 1945, USHMM and Yad Vashem: 67,665 registered in the Dachau system; 43,350 categorized as political prisoners; 22,100 Jews. Yad Vashem: about 30 percent Jewish at liberation. ## 4. Proposed public editorial version

The immutable source version remains untouched. The proposal below is a light
edit of JOM’s “On this day…” voice. It does not add unit names, the corpse
train, Hartheim, or a new death-toll figure merely to lengthen the piece.

Three wording choices change historical meaning and need founder approval
before this text may replace the stored body:

1. Drop “Jewish prisoners as the significant majority.”
2. Soften the 30,000-dead / 30,000-rescued symmetry.
3. Soften “flags from the various Allied nations” to homemade flags.

### Proposed public title

US Liberates Dachau

### Proposed body

On this day, American troops liberated Dachau. The camp had been in operation
for more than a decade. It first held political prisoners, who were forced to
help construct it. In 1942, a crematorium was built there. Prisoners were
subjected to medical experiments against their will, killing or disabling
hundreds of people.

In the days leading up to liberation, thousands of prisoners were forced on a
death march toward Tegernsee, Germany. Nazi authorities also sent large
numbers of prisoners into Dachau from other camps. When the Americans
arrived, prisoners greeted their liberators with flags they had made. About
30,000 prisoners were still in the camp. Tens of thousands had already died
there.
